## Taste · 3.5/5

I had slow-cooked Texas-style beef brisket, served in slices with creamy hummus, juicy red and yellow tomatoes, and a little coriander. There were also these thin, crispy strands that I think might have been shrimp.

The brisket was tender and juicy. The sauce coating it was creamy and a little sweet, with some edamame that I really liked. The best bite was the brisket with a tomato. The acidity balanced the sweetness of the sauce. I usually wouldn’t eat coriander on its own, but lightly dressed with olive oil, it tasted great even by itself.

I also had a baked quesadilla with cheddar, onion, and a little coriander inside, plus some mozzarella on top. It was crispy outside and juicy inside.

To wash it all down, I had Raise a Toast, their house-brewed American pale ale at 4% ABV. It smelled slightly sweet and floral. It tasted crisp, refreshing, and a little bitter, and it went really well with the brisket. The beer was a highlight. The food was decent and mostly lived up to my expectations.

## Atmosphere · 4/5

Half-ton is in Brewtown, a walkable block of food and entertainment by the Snow Brewery. It has a quaint, rustic American brewery feel with a modern Asian twist. There’s concrete everywhere, wood on the walls, and oak and stone countertops. Take out the furniture and you could almost mistake it for a brutalist art museum. I liked the faded steel utensil jars, the kind you might see on an old sailing ship. It was drizzling a little when I came in, which added to the mood.

There’s indoor and outdoor seating. I arrived around six and the tables were already booked, so I sat at the bar. It was a little shallow, so there wasn’t much room for my legs. It got busier around seven, and by the time I was finishing up, the whole place was packed and a little noisy.

## Service · 4.5/5

The staff were friendly and happy to help. I was going to finish with an espresso, but they’d run out.
